# BERYL CLIQUE — 40-SECOND FUNDING PROMO
### Working title: *"Faces on the Call"*
**Runtime:** 40s · **4 × 10s segments** · Runway Gen-3 Alpha (sound-native)
**Purpose:** Investor-facing hero clip. Problem → Solution → Team → Bet.
**Format:** 16:9, cinematic, sound-on. Color arc: cold blue → royal gold.

_Each prompt below is fully self-contained. Paste it exactly into Runway Gen-3 Alpha.
Narration, sound design, camera movement, lighting, and mood are all baked into each prompt.
No post-production VO mix required. Stitch the four 10-second clips using the transitions noted at each seam._

---

## THE 40-SECOND ARC

| # | 0–10s | 10–20s | 20–30s | 30–40s |
|---|-------|--------|--------|--------|
| **Beat** | THE PROBLEM | THE SOLUTION | THE TEAM | THE CATEGORY |

## SEGMENT 1 · 0–10s · THE PROBLEM

**No reference image required.** Marcus is a supporting character — generate him entirely from the prompt.

**RUNWAY PROMPT — paste as-is:**

```
Cinematic 10-second shot. Photorealistic. Ultra-detailed. 16:9 aspect ratio.

SETTING: A dim home office at 2 AM. The room is nearly dark. The only light source is the cold blue-white glow of a laptop screen. There is no overhead light, no lamp on — just that harsh screen light. The desk has a cold aluminum laptop, a half-empty coffee mug with a ring stain on the desk, a tangle of cables, and scattered printed API docs with annotations in the margins. The walls behind are barely visible — dark paint, maybe a whiteboard covered in architectural diagrams that are too dim to read. The room feels like it hasn't seen natural light in days. There is a sense of isolation and technical claustrophobia.

MARCUS: A man in his early 30s. Warm brown skin, medium-dark complexion. Short cropped dark hair, very close cut. Neat light beard stubble — a few days grown, not groomed. He is wearing a charcoal-gray henley, slightly rumpled, collar stretched. His posture is slightly hunched, elbows on desk. His face is exhausted — dark circles under his eyes, jaw loose, eyes glazed as he stares at the screen. He is not angry. He is depleted. He exhales slowly through his nose at the end of the clip.

HIS SCREEN: This is critical. The screen shows a grid of AI agent dashboards. There are NO human faces anywhere on his screen — only gray circles with initials (like "AA", "B1", "G7"), monochrome robot icons, and plain-text terminal windows. The dashboard has columns of "AGENT ID:", "STATUS: IDLE", "LAST RESPONSE: 4m ago", "PROMPT TOKENS: 1,842". Some windows show endless scrolling "assistant: ..." terminal transcripts in green monospace font. One window shows a JSON config file open — raw prompt engineering, cold and mechanical. Another shows a chat thread of the user typing into a blank void and the agent responding with a numbered list. The screen fills his face with cold blue-white light. It looks like a wall. Not a team.

CAMERA: The shot begins slightly wide — over Marcus's left shoulder, showing his silhouette and the full screen. Over the 10 seconds, the camera very slowly, smoothly pushes in — a slow creep over his shoulder toward the screen, getting closer to that wall of faceless agent UIs. By the end of the shot, the screen nearly fills the frame. The push is so subtle it feels like dread accumulating. No shake. Locked off except for the gentle push.

DEPTH OF FIELD: Shallow. Marcus's shoulder in the foreground is slightly soft. The laptop screen is the sharpest point. The room behind him is completely blurred into dark bokeh.

COLOR GRADE: Cold. Deep teal shadows. Blue-white highlights from the screen. Skin tones desaturated. No warmth anywhere in the frame. Feels like 2 AM in January.

FILM LOOK: Fine 35mm film grain over the entire image. Subtle anamorphic lens characteristics — very slight horizontal lens flares from the screen light. Cinematic aspect ratio bars (thin black letterbox top and bottom).

IN-CLIP AUDIO: The room is almost silent. You can hear the faint hum of a laptop fan. A mechanical clock ticks somewhere off-screen — slow and steady, marking time. Faint, sparse single piano notes play — one note every few seconds, low and cold, not a melody, just presence. There is no music. There is no human voice in the background. Then: a male narrator speaks, calm and unhurried, an Apple or Mercedes voice-over quality — measured, intelligent, not dramatic. He speaks over approximately the first 7 seconds: "AI agents were supposed to work like a team. Instead — a wall of terminals. And nobody home." On the word "home" — a pause. Then Marcus exhales through his nose. Just once. Then silence for the final beat before the transition.

MOOD: Technical loneliness. The feeling of talking into a void. The feeling of building something alone at 2 AM and realizing the tools you are using have no soul. Quiet dread. Exhaustion. Not anger — resignation.
```

**TRANSITION OUT → SEGMENT 2:**
In the final 0.5 seconds of this clip, the cold blue frame begins to bleed — a warm gold light bleeds in from the right edge of frame, as if a door is opening somewhere. This is the cross-dissolve seam into Segment 2. Duration: 0.7 seconds.

There is genuine warmth in her face. She is not performing warmth. She is warm. She tilts her head just slightly as she speaks, a natural listening gesture. She speaks clearly, at a conversational pace, not fast, not slow.

```
AMANDA'S LIGHTING: The light on her face is warm gold. Soft key light from her left (viewer's right). It is the opposite of the cold screen light in Segment 1. Where Marcus's scene was blue-white and hard, Amanda's panel is gold and soft. The contrast is immediate and visceral.

CAMERA: The camera is slowly, gently pulling back from the screen — not dramatically, just gradually widening. As the shot progresses, we begin to see more of Marcus in the foreground — his silhouette, the relaxing of his shoulders. The screen with Amanda on it is always the brightest, warmest thing in the frame. DEPTH OF FIELD: Shallow to medium. Amanda in the screen panel is the sharpest point. Marcus's face in the foreground is slightly soft. The room is bokeh.

COLOR GRADE: The grade evolves within this clip. The first 2 seconds are still carrying the cold blue-teal from the previous shot. By second 4, warmth is taking over — amber creeping in from the screen. By second 8, the frame is dominantly warm gold. This is not an abrupt change — it is a slow bloom, like sunrise entering a cold room.

FILM LOOK: Same 35mm grain as Segment 1. Anamorphic characteristics — but now the lens flares from the screen are warm gold instead of cold blue. Cinematic letterbox bars.

IN-CLIP AUDIO: The terminal beeps and clock ticking from Segment 1 stop completely and immediately the moment Amanda's face appears on screen. Complete silence for one beat. Then: a single warm piano note blooms — full, resonant, unhurried. It sustains. Then soft strings enter beneath it, very quietly at first. The male narrator — same voice as Segment 1, same calm quality — continues the narration, speaking over approximately the first 5 seconds: "What if the agent had a face. A name. And she already knew yours." His tone has changed from tired resignation to quiet revelation. Then a breath of silence. Then AMANDA speaks, in her own voice — a warm, clear, mid-alto voice, calm, not performing, genuinely speaking to Marcus: "Hey — I'm Amanda. Tell me what you're building." That line takes approximately 3 seconds. It ends on the word "building" which lands with warmth, not a question — she is already ready to help. A soft ambient meeting-room sound is underneath: the faint shift of a chair, a soft breath — the sound of a real person on the other end of a call.

MOOD: The turn. The moment of relief when you realize something is different this time. The feeling of being seen — a face, a name, a direct gaze. It should feel like the moment you realize the tool you're using is actually a person. Not magic. Not AI. A person.
```
